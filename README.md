# How I work

Research Analyst by title, but most of my work sits in DevRel, across technology, ecosystem, and business. **I turn loosely defined problems into things people can actually build or use.**

Based in Seoul. I spend most of my time working with Web3 and privacy teams, helping their technology land in the Korean market, particularly with institutions and enterprises.

## What I do

- **I don't stay in one lane.** I run research, analyze data, and draw out the insights that matter. I read technical docs, test APIs, and write demo code. I help shape strategy, run events online and offline, give talks, and teach.
- **I don't stop at strategy.** I take it down to the flow, the copy, the criteria, and the thing that actually ships. Wherever the gap is, I try to fill it.

## How I think

- **I start by defining the goal.** Before deciding what to do, I want to be clear about what we are actually trying to change or achieve. A well-executed plan is not very useful if it is solving the wrong problem.
- **I map out the possible ways to get there.** I look at each option in terms of expected impact, time, and resources, then use those trade-offs to decide what deserves attention first.
- **I think about sequence, not just priority.** Which actions depend on others, which ones make the next step easier, and how the pieces need to fit together to reach the broader goal.
- **I execute deliberately.** Once the direction is clear, I put a lot into plans and documentation upfront: likely questions, dependencies, constraints, and edge cases. Resolving them early usually saves time later.
- **I measure what happens against what I expected.** When something does not work, I preserve the reasoning and the outcome rather than starting from zero the next time. If the result falls short, I revisit the assumptions, form a new hypothesis, and test the next approach.
- **Getting the goal right is the most important part.** When the goal itself is unclear or changing, I would rather resolve that first than optimize execution around an assumption that may no longer hold.

## How I use AI

The way I use AI is closely tied to how I think.

- **I do not use AI to replace judgment.** I design it to support each stage of my own reasoning process: defining the goal, exploring possible approaches, comparing trade-offs, planning execution, testing assumptions, and deciding what to do next.
- **It plugs into each stage.** Defining a problem, I use it to surface assumptions, alternative interpretations, and missing context. Exploring options, to widen the search space. Prioritizing, to gather evidence, compare scenarios, and pressure-test the criteria. Executing, across research, analysis, drafting, coding, and testing that can run in parallel. When the outcome differs from what I expected, to revisit the assumptions and develop the next hypothesis.
- **I do not adapt my thinking to AI.** I try to make AI adapt to the way I think and work.
- **I treat my AI setup as something I keep improving.** It is not a static tool that gets better simply because I wrote a better prompt. Context, memory, rules, feedback, tools, and workflows all affect how well it performs, so I keep refining them over time.
- **When the output is weak, I look at the system, not just the answer.** I make my decision criteria, working patterns, and project context explicit, and I ask what was missing around the answer. Sometimes the fix is better context. Sometimes a new rule, a different workflow, or a different model.
- **I use multiple models for the same reason.** GPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok, and others have different strengths, so I assign work by what each is good at: one for research, another to challenge the reasoning, another for execution or validation. For important work, I cross-check conclusions across models rather than relying on a single system.
- **I build around my own workflow.** When useful, I connect MCPs, plugins, skills, agents, and other tools, or build small pieces of infrastructure. I also run work in parallel, with different sessions or agents handling separate tasks before their outputs come back into one decision process.
- **The point is not simply to work faster.** It is to preserve my own reasoning and judgment while expanding how much I can research, test, build, and execute at once. I see AI as a system I can keep refining and configuring around the way I work.
